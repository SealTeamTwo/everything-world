# Land review: music, activities and small moments (2026-11-15)

A planning read of every land, written overnight to talk through together. **Nothing here is built or decided.** For each land:
- **Music and scenery:** does the scenery fit all of its music? Which artists and songs don't, and why (like the Post-Punk Docklands).
- **Main activity:** one or two ideas for its game inside the game. Each is tied to the land's music, can be dropped into and walked away from, works alone or with friends, and is different each time by chance.
- **Small things:** small, fluid moments: a drink, a seat with a view, something to fiddle with.
- **Twist** (some lands): something no other land has.

The vision behind it is at the top of IDEAS.md ("The lobby game"). Mini golf stays reserved for the Yacht Rock Riviera.

**How to use it:** skim, mark what excites you and what doesn't fit, and we'll choose a first activity pilot and the lands to rework from there. Ideas are meant to be argued with.

# Land planning review, batch A

### The Everything Hub

**Music and scenery:** Golden late afternoon with the light tinted per song already covers a station that plays anything. Fits.

**Main activity:** None of its own (fixes only). It could be where the others pay off: a "trophy wall" on the dais that lights one tile for each land whose main activity you have finished, matching the "songs unlock in the Hub" idea.

**Small things:**
- A coffee and pastry cart by the stands (cups to carry and sit with), so meeting here before heading out has a drink in it.
- Make the departures board a real departure, a musical bypass: a quiet boat or pod at the jetty that takes you to the row you pick. You hear only the Hub's station on the way.
- The quiet bench gets a flask of tea someone left.

**Twist:** Skip. The Hub is the one place meant to stay the same.

### The Jam Band Commonwealth

**Music and scenery:** Blue-hour festival fits almost all of it, including the bluegrass-leaning Phish ("Rocky Top", "Old Home Place") and Yonder Mountain, which sit fine in woods. Fits.

**Main activity:**
- **Setlist Bingo at the Lot.** Phish fans live for guessing setlists. A tent sells you a card of eight songs picked at random from the station's tracks. As the radio plays one, its square stamps itself. Line up a row before the hour's out and the vendor hands you a glowing pin. Friends each have their own card and can compare. Every card is different because the shuffle is.
- **The Glowstick Wars.** During "Harry Hood" (and rarely other songs) crates of glowsticks appear on the lawn. Throw them over the crowd; the crowd throws back, and the sky fills with arcs. You can join, watch or ignore it.

**Small things:**
- A cooler of cans by the campground fire; take one and sit on a log.
- Hula hoops and spinning poi at the mirror dome.
- A chalk board at the gate where the "show of the night" (the last Phish song played) is written up.

**Twist:** Gamehendge as a living story on the clock. Each day the Lizards and Wilson's guards hold a different part of the woods, and the Book moves. Some days the Book is "freed" and the Rhombus glows gold all day.

### Kingdom of Classic Rock

**Music and scenery:** Golden, regal light suits the Beatles, Elvis and Buddy Holly. But the land has nowhere for its menace: "Gimme Shelter" and "Sympathy For The Devil", "Heroin" by The Velvet Underground, "I Want You (She's So Heavy)", "Riders on the Storm", "In the Ghetto" and Bowie's "Lazarus" all play into the same buttercup sunshine. "Kokomo" and "Tequila" are beach songs (the Pier helps). The war below gives the dark songs a home: storm over the front lines, the green still sunny.

**Main activity:**
- **The Siege Engines.** At each front line, each side's camp has a trebuchet. Load a record from the crate and fling it at the enemy's banners on their earthworks; knock three down before the song ends. The wind changes with every song and blows the shots off course. Friends can crew one engine together or fight from opposite camps.

**Small things:**
- A pint at the Royal Rock carried out to the green benches.
- A field kitchen behind the lines: a stew pot, tin mugs of tea.
- A scout's spyglass on the castle wall that shows where the front is right now.

**Twist (the war, fleshed out):** Four great houses hold the land: The Beatles, Led Zeppelin, Bowie and Hendrix. The Stones, The Who and Queen are smaller houses that switch sides. Their colours are told apart by pattern and brightness (stripes, chevrons, stars, solid), not hue. Each house's territory is sized by how many of its songs played in the last two hours on the station's own clock, so everyone sees the same map. The house whose song is playing pushes its front forward over the song. Along the borders are hay-bale trenches, tents, cannons firing confetti and soldiers in band-coat colours. Song events:
- "Revolution 1": a mutiny; the soldiers swap banners.
- "Golden Slumbers" and "Carry That Weight": the soldiers lie down and sleep.
- "Immigrant Song": a longship raid up the coast.
- "Gimme Shelter": the storm sits over the front.
- "Something": a truce; soldiers dance in no-man's-land.

### The Psychedelic Wilds

**Music and scenery:** The hot gold desert fits Tame Impala, Khruangbin and the Tuareg desert guitar of Mdou Moctar and Etran de L'Aïr perfectly. The heavy end doesn't fit: Pagan Altar's "Judgement of the Dead" (doom), Elder's "Sanctuary", Earthless, Black Mountain, Boris's droning "Farewell" and Wolfmother's "Joker And The Thief" get the same lavender daytime. Give the heavy songs a dust storm: a brown-violet haze rolls in, the mushrooms glow harder and the mesas go dark against it. QOTSA already has a dusk of its own.

**Main activity:**
- **The Mirage.** Every song, a mirage appears somewhere out in the desert: a shimmering palace, a giant floating tomato, a sea with a ship. It drifts away as you approach. Corner it (friends from two sides works best) before the song ends and it turns solid for the rest of the song, so you can walk inside. The spot and what appears are drawn by chance.

**Small things:**
- A cold drink from MOTORIK's vending machine, sipped on the forecourt kerb.
- Sandboarding down a big dune, with the board to carry back up.
- Mint tea poured from the brass pot in the Oasis tent.

**Twist:** The shifting desert. Places stay put, but the dunes, the mushroom groves and the colour bands on the mesas move to new spots each day on the shared clock, so the walk between them is never the same twice.

### The Folk Woods

**Music and scenery:** The misty morning suits most of it. But a cluster of night songs gets a sunrise: "rises the moon" by Liana Flores, "Breathe (2 AM)" by Anna Nalick, "Werewolves of London", "Lover, You Should've Come Over". Kate Bush's "Running Up That Hill" and Fiona Apple's "Fast As You Can" are urgent, not gentle. "Sweet Caroline" is a singalong. Suggestion: the night songs draw the wood into lamp-lit evening, with cabin windows bright. "Sweet Caroline" gets the whole village singing on the green.

**Main activity:**
- **The Post Round.** The general store hands you a satchel of three letters for random addresses: a cabin, the fire tower, the lighthouse keeper, a canoe moored across the lake. Walk, paddle or ride the Peace Train to deliver them. Each one lights a lamp in a window and earns a line back from whoever lives there. Friends split the round. The addresses and letters are new every time.
- **Canoe crossing:** paddle a canoe out to a tiny island in the lake where something different has washed up each day.

**Small things:**
- Hot cider from the press, in a mug, on a porch rocker.
- Skipping stones off the footbridge.
- Toast bread on a stick at a campfire.

**Twist:** A village in the Old Giants. Platforms ring the sequoias high above the pines, joined by rope bridges and reached by a rope-and-pulley lift: a few cabins, a lookout, a tiny store. This is different from the Jam Band's single treehouse.

### The Yacht Rock Riviera

**Music and scenery:** The endless sunset fits the smooth core. Some songs drift: "Edge of Seventeen" and "Go Your Own Way" are driving and restless; Chicago's "25 or 6 to 4" is heavy brass rock; "Tusk" is a marching-band oddity. Night songs ("In the Air Tonight", "Night Moves", "Dancing in the Moonlight") already have moons. A small tweak would fit them: night songs let the sun set and the bay light up with boat lamps and promenade bulbs.

**Main activity:**
- **The Riviera Mini Golf** (reserved, east meadow): nine holes, one per artist.
  - Through the arches of the Hotel California ("you can check out", but the ball loops back once).
  - Up Elton's yellow brick road.
  - Round TOTO's little Kilimanjaro.
  - Into a giant blender (Margaritaville).
  - Over Steely Dan's koi bridge.
  - Through the Danger Zone jet's afterburner.

  The flags move to new cups each day. One "hit of the hour" hole changes with the song playing. Scorecards at the clubhouse; friends take turns.
- **A regatta:** a tiny sailboat race round three buoys on the bay. The wind shifts per song.

**Small things:**
- A cocktail from the Yacht Club bar, carried to the terrace.
- Fishing off the end of the piano pier.
- Feeding the gulls on the promenade.

### The Prog Marches

**Music and scenery:** The eclipse fits Pink Floyd, King Crimson, Rush and Steven Wilson. Zappa's goofy songs ("Montana", "Po-Jama People", "Peaches En Regalia") and "Blinded By The Light" are playful, but their pieces carry the joke already. Fits well.

**Main activity:**
- **The Concept Album.** Each day a different short story (assembled from parts: a hero, a lost thing, a strange place, an ending) is scattered across the marches as numbered tracks on stone tablets: side one, then side two. Find them in order, and the last one sends you to the gatefold, a place where the story ends with an event. Friends can hunt different sides at once. The story is never quite the same.
- **The orrery puzzle:** align the planets on their brass arms to the day's alignment, chalked on a slate by the observatory, and the eclipse briefly passes.

**Small things:**
- Tea at the Tea Garden actually pours.
- A pair of headphones on a long cord at the Listening Room armchair, so you can sit in the sweet spot.
- A hot pie at the ferry jetty.

**Twist:** The Prism's six ribbons become walkable roads of light: each one is a bridge into the sky to its own floating isle. One reaches all the way to the Hub, a musical bypass that plays only the Marches' station.

### The Iron Range

**Music and scenery:** The storm and forge fit the heart. A cosmic strand sits oddly under storm clouds: Rainbow's "Stargazer", Blood Incantation's "The Stargate", First Fragment's classical prelude and Metallica's "Orion". Suggestion: those songs part the storm into a clear, violently starry sky over the peak. "Whiskey In The Jar" and Danzig's "Mother" sit fine in the roadhouse spirit.

**Main activity:**
- **Into the Deep.** One mine portal on the Forge Peak leads down into a mine that is laid out fresh each day (underground). Carry a lantern; find ore veins, an old riff stone, a lost miner. The deeper levels grow hotter and redder until you reach a molten cavern under the forge. Ore you bring up can be struck into an ingot at Hammertown's anvil. Friends go down roped together (light is shared).
- **Round 115:** a co-op hold-out in Elena Siegman's bunker during "115" and "The One". Board up the windows as pale silhouettes shuffle in out of the mist; the mystery crate rolls something random.

**Small things:**
- A mug of something hot from the miners' kettle at Hammertown.
- Warm your hands at a brazier (sit spots round them).
- Kick the Rusty Nail's jukebox, which already exists, for a drink at the bar.

**Twist:** The mine is the Range's other half, a land below a land.

### The Southern Rock Bayou

**Music and scenery:** The golden-green swamp fits nearly everything. "Fortunate Son" is an angry protest song in a lazy afternoon; it could bring a sudden hot wind and thunder over the river. Otherwise it fits.

**Main activity:**
- **The Trap Line.** Take a jon boat out and set crawfish traps in the channels and the Wisp Swamp. Come back a few songs later and see what's in them:
  - mostly crawfish;
  - sometimes a boot or a lost harmonica;
  - rarely a gator's gold tooth or a bottle with a song title in it.

  Bring the haul to the Fish Camp boil, and the table fills up for everyone. Slow, social, different every time.
- **The Airboat Run:** a buoyed course through the channels that changes each day. Race the clock or a friend.

**Small things:**
- Sweet tea in a mason jar from the General Store's ice chest.
- A ceiling fan on the juke joint porch you can turn up.
- A paper boat of boiled peanuts.

**Twist:** The river breathes. On the shared clock the water rises for a day or two, flooding the low roads (plank bridges become the only way), opening new channels to paddle, and leaving the lagoons wide. Then it falls back to mud flats with herons.

### The Hair Metal Strip

**Music and scenery:** Neon dusk fits. "Janie's Got a Gun" is dark next to the silliness, and the power ballads ("I Don't Want to Miss a Thing", "November Rain") already have a mood of their own. Fits.

**Main activity:**
- **The Shred-Off at the Club.** Two players take the amp-head stage for a call-and-response guitar duel. The game plays a random lick; you answer it on the keys; the crowd raises lighters for whoever's cleaner. Win three rounds and the pyro fires for you. Solo players duel a house guitarist. The licks are random every time.
- **Drag night on the Desert Highway:** two hot rods at a Christmas-tree light. Random opponents from the Strip.

**Small things:**
- A neon-blue slushie from the motel's machine, drunk on a lounger.
- Spin the motel sign.
- A Polaroid booth on the Strip.

**Twist:** Battle of the venues. Each night one venue on the Strip is "headlining", with a queue, the brightest marquee and its band's songs pumped louder. It is picked on the clock, and the crowd moves to it.

### The Bluegrass Hollows

**Music and scenery:** The summer holler fits. "Rye Whiskey", "Black River" and "Constant Sorrow" are darker, but are already handled with rain. Fits.

**Main activity:**
- **The County Fair.** A rotating fair in the low meadow, a different contest each day on the clock:
  - a frog-jumping derby;
  - a pie table to judge;
  - horseshoes;
  - a hog-calling holler;
  - a sack race;
  - a greased-pig chase.

  Winners get a blue rosette pinned at the general store. Friends compete or cheer from hay bales. Something new every visit.
- **Pickin' in:** sit in one of the seven rockers and one floating instrument comes down to you. Tap along to the station's beat, and the more chairs filled, the faster the circle plays.

**Small things:**
- A ladle of cold spring water, or lemonade, at the store.
- Corn on the cob off the fire.
- A porch dog that follows you a bit.

**Twist:** Skip. The fair rotating daily is enough.

### Arena Rock Ridge

**Music and scenery:** Night stadium fits. "Sunday Bloody Sunday" is political, and "Can't Fight This Feeling" and "Sister Christian" are ballads, but the lighters cover them. Fits.

**Main activity:**
- **The Load-In and the Show.** Before a song's last stretch the stage is empty. Friends carry road cases, raise the truss with the chain winch and plug in the amps against the clock. Then step onto the marked spots (singer, two guitars, drums), and the crowd follows you: jump and the flame jets fire, raise your arms and the lighters go up, walk the runway and the searchlights follow. The crowd sometimes demands an encore at random.
- **Crowd-surf:** lie on the field crowd and be passed toward the stage.

**Small things:**
- A hot dog and a soda from the concession stand at the gate.
- A tailgate seat with a cooler.
- A foam finger to carry.

**Twist:** On tour. Each day the stadium's stage is dressed for a different band's tour (Journey's wheel, Boston's ship, BÖC's reaper), and the tour posters change.

### Metalcore Caldera

**Music and scenery:** Neon volcanoes fit the heavy core. A brighter, hopeful strand gets nothing of its own: Turnstile's sunny hardcore, One Step Closer's "Blue Hour", Silverstein's "My Heroine", Our Last Night's "Beautiful Things", Jon Keith's "YOU CAN ALWAYS COME BACK HOME". So does a goofy one: Breathe Carolina's "Wooly", Wendy's, Broadway. Suggestion: the hopeful songs bring a "blue hour". The ash stops falling and a pale dawn sky opens over the rim for the song, the cathartic after-the-breakdown feeling.

**Main activity:**
- **The Wall of Death.** At the breakdown on the clock, the circle pit splits in two at the arena. Stand in one half; when the drop hits, charge. The timing of the charge (too early or late, you get bumped) and a randomised breakdown point make it different every song.
- **Crowd-surf over the barrier:** the crowd carries you to the stage.

**Small things:**
- A late-night drive-thru window at the Wendy's sign: fries and a frosty to carry.
- An energy-drink vending machine by the arena.
- Sit on a toppled amp.

**Twist:** The Sulphur Heart (from the friend's idea). A field of yellow sulphur crust in the hottest hollow, with real flickering pale blue flames. Note that the lava is already cyan, violet and magenta, so the blue fire must read differently: low, flickering and paired with bright yellow crust. That gives the strongest blue/yellow contrast in the Caldera.

### The Alt Rock Moors

**Music and scenery:** The moody English moor fits Radiohead, Pulp, Blur, The Cranberries and Oasis. A big Californian, sunny strand doesn't fit: "All Star" and "I'm A Believer" by Smash Mouth, "Can't Stop", Weezer's "Island In The Sun", Hootie, 311's "Sunset in July", No Doubt, Incubus's "Drive". They play into grey skies. Suggestion, like the Docklands: the sunny 90s-radio songs break the clouds into a hot summer afternoon over the moor, while Radiohead keeps its uncanny grey.

**Main activity:**
- **The Pub Quiz at the Lamb & Antenna.** Every night the publican runs a quiz drawn from the playlist: name the artist from three plaque words, which land does this song live in, finish the lyric title. Friends team up at a table with a pencil. The questions are random every time, and the winners' team name goes up on the chalkboard until the next quiz.
- **Fog orienteering:** a map card and five checkpoints on the moor, new each day, best done when the fog rolls in.

**Small things:**
- Fish and chips in paper from a van in the village.
- A packet of crisps at the bar.
- Sheltering in the bus shelter when it rains, watching it.

**Twist:** Skip. The weather following the song is enough.

### The Punk Badlands

**Music and scenery:** Harsh noon fits the pop-punk and ska. The emo and ballad strand doesn't fit the sun: Mayday Parade's "Miserable At Best", "Hey There Delilah", "Death of a Bachelor", "I'm a Loner Dottie, a Rebel". Patti Smith's "Gloria" is 70s New York art-punk, not the suburbs. Suggestion: the sad songs turn the sky to evening on the last day of school, streetlights on and kids sitting on kerbs.

**Main activity:**
- **S.K.A.T.E. in the Pool.** Grab a board from the pile and drop in. Each song sets a random challenge ("grind the coping", "air over the ladder", "three wall rides"). Friends play S.K.A.T.E.: copy the trick or take a letter. Easy controls; fall off and you're standing at the edge.
- **The Bike Gang:** BMXs to ride round the cul-de-sacs, with a random race route each day.

**Small things:**
- A slushie from a gas station, drunk on the car on blocks.
- Sit on the roof of a car.
- Play basketball on the turning-circle hoop.

**Twist:** Every house is a bedroom. The roofless shells become a street of teenage bedrooms to walk into, each with posters and a stereo playing tinny along, and each dressed by chance.

### The Math Rock Escarpment

**Music and scenery:** Autumn above and still post-rock below fit well. A few bedroom/lofi tracks are bright and cute rather than autumnal: saddyowner's "Minecraft", Lilac Stranger's "Bubble Party!", sincerely's "Glazey", Just Neighbors, Chinese Football's "Electronic Girl". They sit fine among the bubbles and lights. Fits.

**Main activity:**
- **Leaf Boats.** At the head of each stream a basket of folded paper boats. Drop one in and run alongside it as it rides the currents toward the falls; snags, eddies and the falls' drop are random every time. Friends race their boats. Fastest gets their boat pinned on the cliff-top house's window.
- **The Scarp Descent:** a via ferrata of timber treads and chains down the face of the escarpment beside the biggest fall, ending in the mist pool.

**Small things:**
- A hot chocolate from the yatai.
- Jump into a leaf pile.
- A bench at the cliff edge with a thermos.

**Twist:** Skip.

### The Grunge Rainwood

**Music and scenery:** Fits well; the special-cases already cover "No Rain" and "Black Hole Sun". The one gap is the loud, bright radio-grunge side: Foo Fighters' "My Hero", Live's "All Over You", Bush's "Everything Zen", Silverchair's "Freak" and Smashing Pumpkins' "Zero" play under the same drizzle as "Nutshell". This lines up with the owner's own note (more colour, more energy). A fix that stays in the land's look: on loud songs the rain stops and low wet sunlight comes in sideways under the cloud, so the puddles and the wet road shine.

**Main activity:** *The Log Drive.* Logs come down the mill race into the pool. Jump on one and ride it down the river's rapids to the sea, steering and hopping between logs. Jams, fallen trees and spray are placed fresh each day on the shared clock. A chalk time board hangs on the mill wall, and friends can race or heckle from the plank bridges. Second idea: *Big Fella sightings.* Take an old camcorder from the coffee shack and catch him on tape. He turns up somewhere different every song, the shots come out blurry, and the best ones get pinned up in the shack.

**Small things:** Coffee in a paper cup from the shack, steaming in the rain. Climb a fire lookout and sit at the top with a view of the falls. A chalk set list outside the mill showing the next three songs on the station.

### The Post-Punk Docklands

**Music and scenery:** Already agreed: the time of day follows the song. Post-punk gets deep night, new wave, pop and ska ("Vacation", "Our House", "Take on Me", "Come On Eileen") get a bright afternoon, and noise and art rock (Melt-Banana, Black Country, New Road, Maruja, Sonic Youth) get a stormy dusk. The ideas below are written to work with all three hours.

**Main activity:** *Load the Ship.* Climb into a container crane's cab and stack containers onto the moored ship before it sails. A new manifest, stack pattern and departure time come with each sailing, every few songs on the shared clock. One friend drives the crane while others call it in from the quay; at night you load by sodium light, in the afternoon in full sun. Second idea: *Flyposting.* Pick up a roll of gig posters for tonight's bill (the station's coming songs) and paste them over walls and hoardings before the next bill arrives. Your posters stay up for the day for friends to find.

**Small things:** A dockers' tea hut on the quay; it shows only at its hour (a mug at dawn and afternoon, shut at night). Skim stones off the breakwater. A photo booth that prints a strip in black and white at night and in colour by day.

**Twist:** The hour system itself. Each artist "owns" an hour, so the land is never the same twice in a row.

### The Djent Spires

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. Bright, precise and technical suits Plini, Polyphia, Animals As Leaders and Intervals, and Sleep Token already has its dark corner. Opeth's gentle "Windowpane" and Coheed's "Welcome Home" are a little earthier, but nothing clashes.

**Main activity:** *The Fractal Climb.* The floating cubes that turn in grids become a climb up to the citadel's gold heart. With each new song the grid snaps into a new route generated from the shared clock, and the cubes move in that song's meter (3 against 4, 7s), so you jump on the beat. Friends climbing together see the same route, and others watch from the hex plaza. Reaching the heart lights a facet of the citadel for the rest of the day. Second idea: *Polyrhythm Duets.* Two players at two crystal pads each tap one voice of a polyrhythm (3 against 4, 5 against 7); when they lock together, the Tesla coils fire.

**Small things:** Sit on a slow-turning floating cube and ride it round. A crystal glass harp to play with wet fingers. Spring water in a crystal cup at a white stone fountain.

**Twist:** The Spires grow. On the shared clock a new crystal spire rises a little each day somewhere on the terraces, so regulars notice new shapes.

### The J-Rock Isles

**Music and scenery:** Fits very well; the anime city at dusk suits the anime themes, rock and vocaloid-style songs. The rhythm-game electronic tracks (Feryquitous, technoplanet's "Megastructure", DEATHNYANN, 楽音's remix) want a bit more neon and arcade, which the city can carry. "The Real Folk Blues" already has its blues bar.

**Main activity:** *Stamp Rally.* Like the real ones at Japanese stations. Each day the shared clock hides six stamp stations (in a shopfront, on a rooftop, at the shrine, on the train, at Cat Hill), each with its own rubber stamp design. Fill your card for a small prize from the Isle Mart clerk. Friends can split up or hunt together, and the route is new every day. Second idea: *Rooftop Run.* A cat runs off with your bento across the rooftops, and you chase it over water tanks, planks and billboards. The cat's route changes each time, like an opening-sequence chase.

**Small things:** A ramen cart under a paper lantern with three stools and steam. A sticker-photo booth (purikura) that frames you and your friends. Sit at the school rooftop fence at dusk while the train goes by.

**Twist:** *Today's episode.* The shared clock gives every day an episode title and a type (Festival Day, Rainy Day, Sports Day, Beach Episode, Test Week). The type changes what happens: stalls up or down, students in uniforms or robes, rain, the track marked out.

### The Oddball Backroads

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. The winking, slightly-off Americana suits Pixies, Ween, Violent Femmes, Beck, Flaming Lips and They Might Be Giants. The gentler songs (Wilco, MJ Lenderman, Silver Jews) suit the hazy afternoon too.

**Main activity:** *The Trade-Up.* At the diner you're handed a paperclip. Trade it with residents along the roads (the motel clerk, the keeper, the saucer pilot) for something slightly better, and keep going until you own something absurd (the leaning water tower's deed, the giant head's spare eye). Each day the shared clock deals new wants and offers, so the chain and the ending change; friends can trade with each other too. Second idea: *Flamingo Round-Up.* The lawn flamingos have gotten loose and wander as a flock. Herd them back into the pasture before the song ends; the flock behaves differently every time.

**Small things:** The old gas pump pours lemonade into a paper cup. A payphone at a crossroads that rings through to a random resident. Sit on a pickup's hood at the crop circle and watch the saucer's lights.

**Twist:** *Open Today.* Each day a few attractions put up CLOSED and one secret new one opens, and one road sign quietly points somewhere wrong (it's right).

### The Nu Metal Quarry

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. The grey overcast and the 2000s car-radio feel suit the earnest choruses. The tender ballads ("Far Away", "Here Without You", "The Reason", "With Arms Wide Open") might warm the light a touch, like a sunset through the dust, rather than staying flat grey.

**Main activity:** *Geode Run.* After a shot at the Blast Bench, the fallen rocks scatter across the bench. Carry them to the crusher shed and crack them on the anvil. Contents come by chance: quartz, amethyst, a fossil, a 2003 flip phone, a burned CD, and very rarely glowing green kryptonite. A shelf in the Crib shows the day's best finds for everyone. Friends can take turns on the plunger and hunt rocks together. Second idea: *Haul Shift.* Drive the giant haul truck up the ramp and tip at the crusher, against a daily quota on a whiteboard; the haul road's mud and rockfalls change each shift.

**Small things:** An energy drink from a dented vending machine at the gate. Sit at the top of the haul truck's ladder looking down into the pit. Skim stones on the Blue Hole.

**Twist:** *The pit gets deeper.* On the shared clock a new bench is dug each week, slowly uncovering things (a buried car, an old shaft, a seam of crystal).

### The Power Metal Peaks

**Music and scenery:** Fits well; the fierce north and kawaii south already cover the split between DragonForce and Sabaton on one side and BABYMETAL and LADYBABY on the other. Alestorm and Wind Rose have the cove and the mine.

**Main activity:** *The Dragon's Hoard.* The castle's dragon sleeps on a heap of gold. Sneak in and grab treasure; the dragon wakes when it breathes fire, on the shared clock, so you learn the song's rhythm and run. The loot (coins, crowns, a pink flying V) is drawn fresh each time, and a friend can play lookout from the battlements. Haul it to the Mead Hall and add it to the table. Second idea: *Broadsides at the Cove.* Man the pirate ship's cannons against barrels and targets bobbing in the swell (never the rubber duck, or the crew boos).

**Small things:** Strike the anvil in time at a forge to sharpen a sword. Sit by a brazier atop a ruined watchtower facing the sunset. Pull the sword from the arrival stone: it never comes out, except once in a very long while by chance, with fanfare for everyone there.

**Twist:** The sword in the stone, above, as the land's one-in-a-thousand legend.

### The Indie Downs

**Music and scenery:** Mostly fits; the music-led weather does a lot. Two urban, after-dark songs sit a little oddly in the sunny countryside: Interpol's "Rest My Chemistry" and "If You Really Love Nothing", and Franz Ferdinand's "Take Me Out". # Batch C: land planning review

### The Ambient Mists

**Music and scenery:** Fits well: a foggy home-video meadow suits Boards of Canada, Marconi Union, Tycho and most of the rest. Two songs run hotter than the scene: Aphex Twin's "Omgyjya-Switch7" (fast, choppy drill'n'bass) and Shpongle's "Divine Moments of Truth" (busy psytrance). The existing colour swirl covers Shpongle; "Omgyjya" could make the tape wobble hard and stutter instead of drifting softly.

**Main activity:** *Signal hunt.* Each session a hidden broadcast starts somewhere in the fog: in a hay bale, a dead TV, the boat or the radio tower, picked by chance from the shared clock. Carry the little picnic radio and the static clears as you get close; with friends you can spread out and call "warmer". At the source you find a cassette. Put it in the TV on the picnic blanket and every TV in the land plays a short, faded "home video" of the land itself (the swing with nobody on it, sheep, the lake), a different clip each time, with rare ones to collect. A Boards of Canada-style number station could give a hint now and then.

**Small things:** A kite to fly in the meadow, its string glowing in the haze. A thermos and two tin cups on the picnic blanket. Lie down in the long grass (E) and watch the pylon wires and the slow clouds.

**Twist:** The land forgets. When thick fog rolls through (rarely, on the clock), small things have moved when it clears: the blanket has shifted, a TV shows a new channel, a sheep sits on the jetty. The paths and places never move, only the details, like a memory that won't hold still.

### The Four-on-the-Floor Steppe

**Music and scenery:** Mostly fits, but the land's biggest artist doesn't use a four-on-the-floor beat. All four Astronaut songs are dubstep or drumstep remixes ("Champions - WRLD Remix", "War", "Rain"), and so are Rob Gasser's "Ricochet", Flight Facilities' "Crave You - Adventure Club Remix" and Vicetone's "Heartbeat - Rameses B Remix". The everything-on-the-kick clock suits Avicii and Daft Punk but beats wrongly under these. Justice's "Flower In A Hurricane" is a slow psychedelic score, not a club track. Fix: the shared clock switches to half-time for the half-time songs (heavier lights, slower sweeps, a bass-tent feel), and "Flower In A Hurricane" brings a slow wind storm through the flags and tents.

**Main activity:** *The crowd follows the music.* Whichever artist is playing on the station, their little stage is "on": the crowd of residents drifts across the plain to it, lights up its canopy and dances there. Your game is to read the night: go up to the main booth and drop the beat at the right moment (after the build) to pull the crowd back to the Main Stage. A good drop wins people; a bad one loses them. Friends can work rival stages. Requests are shouted at random, and once in a while a "secret set" pops up at an unmarked stage.

**Small things:** Ride the Ferris wheel (today you can only watch it); a gondola stops at the top with a view of every stage. Get a wristband at the gate. Noodles from a food truck, eaten on a hay bale.

**Twist:** A lineup board at the gate shows tonight's set times, made from the shared clock: a different festival every night, the same for every friend.

### The Dubstep Shards

**Music and scenery:** Fits well; the crystal canyon is exactly the music. Of The Trees ("Windhorse", "Dolori") is gentler and meditative, and the crystal tree with prayer flags already gives it a quiet corner.

**Main activity:** *Hold your ground.* A raised slab of black stone stands in front of the Speaker Cliff. On every drop the woofers fire a shockwave that shoves anyone standing on the slab. Stay on it through the drop; the wobble rate changes bar by bar, so the timing of each push is never quite the same. With friends it's king of the hill: crouch to brace, bump each other, last one standing gets a crystal crown that glows until the next drop. Alone, the cliff keeps your record of drops survived. Hitting the sub is the dare: it fires an extra wave whenever someone pushes it.

**Small things:** Shards you can snap off a cluster, carry, and throw: they burst into light where they land. A ledge on top of the Speaker Cliff to sit on with your legs over the edge, looking down on the crowd. A bowl of foam earplugs on the mixing desk (put them in and the light goes soft for a while).

**Twist:** The crystals grow. Over hours on the shared clock the shards slowly creep across the open ground; the biggest drops of the day ("Scary Monsters", "Blackout") shatter the overgrowth back in a burst of light. Friends arriving at different times find the canyon at a different stage of growth.

### The IDM Glitchlands

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. Venetian Snares' "Öngyilkos Vasárnap" (mournful strings over breakcore) is the outlier, and its grey rain already handles it.

**Main activity:** *Debug.* Each session the world spawns a set of bugs, picked by chance from a long list: a tree rendered upside down, a resident with no head, a bench duplicated ten times in a row, a floating chunk of ground with nothing under it, a texture gone pink-and-black, a sheep from the Ambient Mists that wandered in. Pick up the debugger (a giant mouse cursor on a stick) and click each one to patch it; it snaps back with a green flash. A counter on the monolith shows bugs left. Friends race or split the map. Rare bugs are funnier and harder to spot. Corrupting the data at the terminal spawns a fresh wave.

**Small things:** A coffee from Mr. Bill's machine ("Caffeine") that makes your own view glitch for a minute. A spinning office chair at the workstation. A vending machine that drops a random object from anywhere in the world, now and then something nobody has seen yet.

**Twist:** The only land that regenerates. The monolith shows today's seed; the floating blocks, wireframe clutter and half-loaded trees between the paths are rebuilt from it each day, the same for every friend. The Kernel, the Bus, the Stack and the artists never move.

### The Melodic Bass Highlands

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. Noisestorm's "Crab Rave" is a joke song, and the dancing crabs already treat it as one; Vexare's "The Clockmaker" is whimsical, and his clock fits it.

**Main activity:** *Island hopping.* The floating islands rise and fall with the music, so the gaps between them keep changing. Jump from island to island to reach a glowing crystal set on a high one, picked by chance each time. A route that's open in the quiet verse closes when the drop lifts everything, and a new one opens. Friends can race, or one calls out the route from the Sky Stair lookout. Falling isn't a fail: you drift down slowly like a seed. Reaching the crystal sends it up to join the great one over Seven Lions' pool, so the pool's crystal grows brighter as more are brought in that day.

**Small things:** Sit on the edge of a floating island with your legs dangling over the land. A cup of blossom tea on the lookout, by the telescope. Pick a pink blossom and drop it off the lookout; watch it spin all the way down.

**Twist:** Gravity follows the music. On the build, everything (you, petals, lanterns) gets lighter; on the drop, you can jump twice as high for a few seconds. It's the land where the music lifts you off the ground, literally.

### The Liquid Rapids

**Music and scenery:** Half the music is liquid (High Contrast, Netsky, Hybrid Minds, Logistics' "Hayling", London Elektricity's "Life Is Beautiful"), and the summer river suits it perfectly. The other half is urban jungle: Goldie's "Inner City Life", Roni Size's "Brown Paper Bag", Uk Apache's "Original Nuttah", Pendulum's "Witchcraft", Camo & Krooked's "Turn Up". That's tower blocks, railway arches and soundsystems, not willows. Fix, in the Docklands' spirit: the river ends in a city. Below the Weir it runs into a concrete canal under railway arches, with graffiti, a soundsystem stack, a towpath and city lights coming on. The jungle songs turn the light toward that dusk and draw your eye downstream; the liquid songs stay up in the warm evening.

**Main activity:** *Run the river.* Take a rubber ring or a canoe from the bank at the Falls and ride the rapids down the River Walk, under the footbridges, over the Weir and into the city canal. The flow runs on the 174 bpm clock (surges on the beat), side channels open and close by chance, and floating logs or a stray canoe change each run. Friends race and bump; alone, beat your time and find the rare hidden channel.

**Small things:** Skim stones from the stepping stones (the skips light up). A loaf from the mill's window, still warm. A hammock slung between two willows over the water.

### The Sample Gardens

**Music and scenery:** Mostly fits: Bibio, The Avalanches, Four Tet and Wintergatan are this garden. A few tracks are more mall, club or screen than garden: alyzea's "aero plaza" and Dolphin Machine (vaporwave, empty shopping centres), DJ Plead's "pa700" (UK funky club) and tictacto's hyperpop "homesick" flip. **Twist:** The garden samples the world. Every land you've visited leaves a cutting here, a small piece of it growing in a bed: a Docklands street lamp, a crystal from the Shards, a tile from the Glitterlands. Over time the garden becomes a collage of the whole world.

### The Trip-Hop Undercity

**Music and scenery:** Fits well, with one clear exception: Glass Animals' "Heat Waves" is a sunny, nostalgic summer song, not 3 a.m. underground. A stairway up to the street could hold it: a hot orange evening glimpsed through the station entrance, shown only while that song plays. Blue Foundation's "Eyes on Fire (Zeds Dead Remix)" is heavier but suits the dark.

**Main activity:** *The drop.* A spy game: each round, a briefcase is hidden somewhere in the Undercity, chosen by chance. **Twist:** The train leaves. The Undercity is the central station of the world's metro, the musical bypass the owner wants: board the waiting train and ride a dark line to another land's station without hearing the lands between, with this land's slow music in the carriage. The map wall becomes the network map.

### The Trance Steppe

### The Electro Foundry

### Mount Virtuoso

### The Synthwave Coast

The time of day could follow the song: synthwave stays at night, chillwave brings a hazy pastel late afternoon over the same coast, the sun warm and blurred.

**Main activity:** *The night drive.* Get into one of the chrome cars at the diner and drive the Coast Road to Sunset Point. Race the pink car, or just cruise. Each drive brings something different, by chance: a police light in the mirror, a rival car pulling alongside, a shooting star, a hitchhiking resident with one line. Friends drive side by side or ride as passengers. Reach Sunset Point before the song ends for a cut to the sun.

**Small things:** A cassette vending machine: get a random tape and put it in the car. A neon-lit rooftop pool to sit by with a milkshake. A drive-in screen at the edge of the lot showing a grainy loop of the coast.

### The Soul Lowlands

**Music and scenery:** Fits beautifully for the Sunday-best golden hour (Stevie Wonder, Sam Cooke, the gospel). A few songs belong after dark: Nathaniel Rateliff's "S.O.B." is a stomping bar song, Syl Johnson's "Is It Because I'm Black" is heavy and slow, Wendy Rene's "After Laughter (Comes Tears)" is sad. A juke joint across the river (a shack with a tin roof, string lights over the water, a crowd stomping on the boards) would give them a home, and those songs could bring the evening down over the land.

**Main activity:** *Amateur Night at The Royal.* On the shared clock the marquee announces a show. Resident acts come on by chance: a tap dancer, a child singer, a magician, a terrible comedian. The audience (players and residents) cheers or boos, and a booed act gets the hook. Players can take the stage too: a simple call-and-response with the band, where the crowd's reaction depends on timing and luck. Friends can be performer, heckler and audience. Another: the Lowlands Belle actually sails a loop on the river on the clock; be on board when she casts off.

**Small things:** An ice cream cone from the parlour, eaten on the square's benches. A shoeshine stand on Main Street. Join a line dance in the square when "Sir Duke" plays.

### The Fusion Isthmus

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. The blue-hour canal town suits Tom Misch, Yussef Dayes, Ezra Collective and BADBADNOTGOOD. Two tracks pull against it. The Comet Is Coming's "Birth Of Creation" is loud, cosmic, apocalyptic sax-and-synth, nothing like a quiet canal. CASIOPEA's "Domino Line" is bright 1980s Tokyo city-fusion that wants sunshine and neon. Both could get a happening: the sky tears open to stars for "Birth Of Creation", and the quays go briefly sunny and glossy for "Domino Line".

**Main activity:** *Call and Response on the Piano Bridge.* A busker plays a short phrase on the bridge keys, chosen at random from a long list, and you answer it by walking the keys. Each round adds a note. With friends, each person holds one octave, so you have to play it together. Miss and the busker laughs and starts a new phrase. A second idea is *the Lamplighter's Round*: at blue hour a random set of lamps goes out, and you take the ladder and relight them before the rowing boat reaches the end of the canal.

**Small things:** Lean on the bridge railing and drop a coin in the canal; the rowing boat's lantern dips as it passes. Sit on the houseboat roof with your legs over the side. A late bakery hatch with a paper bag of something warm.

**Twist:** The canal is a musical bypass. A water taxi carries you out of town through a quiet tunnel of lights to the next land without passing through the ones in between.

### The Old Jazz Quarter

**Music and scenery:** This is the Docklands problem in miniature. The rainy black-and-white midnight is perfect for "Strange Fruit", "So What", "'Tis Autumn", "Bird's Lament" and "Unforgettable". But a lot of the music is joyful. The swing is "In the Mood", "Minnie the Moocher" and "A-Tisket, A-Tasket". "The Girl From Ipanema" is a sunny beach song. Then there is the brass-band strut of Preservation Hall's "Santiago", the calypso of "St. Thomas", and the soul-jazz party of "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy", "Root Down" and "Compared to What". Idea: the rain stays, but those songs bring the colour up and the town out: doors open, dancers spill onto the cobbles, a band under the marquee.

**Main activity:** *The Late Edition.* Every night the news stand prints a new case, dealt from a deck: who stopped the clock, whose trumpet went missing, who left the long car running. The clues are in phone boxes (answer them), in doorways and from the black-and-white residents. When you name the culprit at the news stand, the case goes on the front page and the clock moves one minute. Friends can split up and share clues.

**Small things:** A hot-chestnut cart under an umbrella. A shoeshine stand to sit at. A coat check at The Blue Mode that gives you a ticket and a hat.

**Twist:** Colour is something the town earns. Each solved case brings one colour back to one street for the night, and the shared clock resets it at dawn.

### The Funk Tropics

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. A few sunny songs sit oddly in the night jungle: Commodores' "Easy" (Sunday morning), Jungle's "Sunshine" and Ginger Root's "Loretta" (bright, retro). A warm golden-dawn happening for these three would cover them.

**Main activity:** *Find Sir Nose.* A sulky character who refuses to dance hides somewhere in the jungle, in a new place each time: up a palm, inside a speaker stack, under a giant shoe. Players take a "bop gun" (a torch from the Mothership's stores) and hunt him. Rustling leaves and his grumbling lead you closer. Light him up and he gives in and dances across the dance floor, and the whole jungle pulses. Friends can split up. A second idea is *Rollerama races*: laps of the rink against the skaters, with mirror balls dropping in as random obstacles.

**Small things:** A juice bar in a hollow trunk serving glowing coconuts. A hammock high in the canopy reached by a vine ladder, looking down at the Mothership. A hat-and-sunglasses mirror in the menswear shop.

**Twist:** The jungle grows to the beat. Plants that pulse long enough grow a little each day on the shared clock, so Funk Street slowly gets swallowed and has to be cut back. That makes it a different shape every week.

### The Far Shores

**Music and scenery:** Mostly fits, but parts of the music have no home here. Tony Bennett's "Body and Soul" is a smoky late-night ballad in a bright blue afternoon. Omar Souleyman's "Warni Warni" (Syrian wedding dabke), Khaled's "Aïcha", Elyanna's "Ganeni" and Fonola Band's "Bella ciao" have no Mediterranean or Levant port of call; the guide's stops are Brazil, Ethiopia, the Sahara and America. A small whitewashed Mediterranean harbour stop would give them a place. A dusk happening for "Body and Soul" would help too, like the one "Sleep Walk" already has.

**Main activity:** *Beat the Tide.* Build a sandcastle together on the beach before the tide comes in. Each piece you place (towers, walls, a moat, shells, a flag) holds the water off a little longer. The tide follows the shared clock, and now and then a rogue wave arrives without warning. The best castle of the day stays standing (shown on a postcard) until the next tide. A second idea is *the kite duel*: the wind changes at random, and friends try to keep their kites up longest.

**Small things:** Pick your next destination at the travel bureau, then sit on the trolley of suitcases and wait for it. A shell to hold to your ear. Mint tea poured from a height at the camp.

**Twist:** A ship calls in each day from a different shore. It is chosen at random, with its own flags, cargo and visitors who wander the Promenade, and it sails at sunset.

### The Delta Crossroads

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. The joyful ones are Fats Domino's "Blueberry Hill", Sister Rosetta Tharpe's "Didn't It Rain" and Big Mama Thornton's "Hound Dog". They sit fine among the porches, and "Didn't It Rain" already has its storm.

**Main activity:** *The Wager at the Crossroads.* When a Robert Johnson song brings the dark down, the tall figure at the head of the shadow offers a game of chance: cut the deck, call high or low, three hands. Win and your guitar (or your orb) carries a faint blue glow until dawn. Lose and Albert King's little black cloud follows you and rains on you alone for a while. Friends can stand round and bet on each other with bottle caps. It only happens at that hour, so it stays special. The rest of the time, the general store's checkers could become a full game against the old man.

**Small things:** Draw a cold bottle from the ice chest and sit on the pickup's tailgate. Put your ear to the rail and hear the train before it comes. Hang a bottle on a bottle tree; it stays there for the day.

**Twist:** The shadow on the west road creeps a little closer to the crossroads each day on the shared clock. On a rare night it reaches the crossroads and the tall figure walks the land.

### Discotopia

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. Die Bokjollers' "CCR Medley" is a beer-hall cover medley, not disco, and is the one odd song. The little paddle wheel already gives it a light corner of its own.

Patterns speed up and change (checkers, spirals, one gold tile to fight over). The last dancer standing gets the crown from "Dancing Queen" for the night. Drop in between rounds; the residents dance too, so it works alone.

**Small things:** Order a cocktail with a paper umbrella at a poolside bar by the villas. Roller-skate the Esplanade (skates to borrow). A photo booth strip that prints four poses and pins itself to a board, so friends' photos pile up through the night.

**Twist:** A rare night when the lighthouse mirror ball breaks free and rolls slowly through town, scattering light over everything, until someone throws the lever to call it home.

### The Afrobeat Coast

**Music and scenery:** Fits well. I Hate My Village's "Tony Hawk of Ghana" is Italian guitar afro-rock, and its skate ramp already gives it a fitting spot.

### The Concert Isles

### The Game Isles
